This is the part I'm most excited about: a first, small version of the project. I treated 3D reference simulations as if they were measured data, and matched them against three dictionaries, built three ways. Each panel is one dictionary. Horizontal is the true radius, vertical is what the matching picked. On the gray line means right. On the left, a 3D dictionary built from new random voxels: 10 of 12 right, and the misses are just the next grid value. In the middle, the 2D recipe with its own direction per vessel: 8 of 12, again only one step off. On the right, the MrVox-style 3B0 dictionary: 3 of 12. It calls a 1 micron vessel 15 microns, and everything small comes out around 7. That's Berman's finding again, but on our sequence, with oxygenation estimated at the same time. Adding noise at an SNR of 100 didn't change the picture. To be clear, this is a toy: fixed blood volume, no T2, and only two voxels per entry. It also uses the total signal, blood plus tissue. Most of the 3B0 error lives in the blood signal; in my runs the tissue-only decay rates were only 3 to 16 percent low. So whether our dictionary includes blood matters a lot, and that's one of my questions at the end.
