Now the two papers Jack keeps pointing me to. The first one is the toolbox paper, from Jacob Chaussé, Avery Berman and Jean Chen. Their point is that BOLD simulations used to live in separate codes that mostly weren't shared. They put the common methods into one Python package, free on GitHub, with tutorial notebooks. They also measured what each choice costs. 2D is faster, 3D is more realistic. A grid voxel runs faster per step but takes about a hundred times more memory. The FFT field works for any shape but isn't exact. And grid diffusion gives a clean, noise-free answer, but gets very memory hungry for big vessels. The figure at the bottom shows how big these costs get: a 3D grid voxel took about an hour to build and fourteen and a half gigabytes of memory, versus a tenth of a gigabyte for the continuous one. Their general advice: use the exact field when you can, and keep enough grid points across each vessel.
