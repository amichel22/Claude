Why 3B0 fails: the blood stops dephasing

Same vessels, two ways to compute the field (my runs). Vessel edges are outlined.

With 3B0, the field is averaged over three directions, so the blood in every vessel sits at the same offset, near zero. Only neighbouring vessels add a little spread.

The blood signal then barely decays: 2–⁠3 per second instead of about 42 in the 3D reference (gradient echo, 29 ms).

Blood is only 2–⁠3% of the voxel but carries at least a quarter of the gradient-echo decay, and over half for capillary-sized vessels in my runs. So the total signal, and the fit, are off.

Why does 3B0 do so badly? I made these two field maps to see it for myself. Same vessels, two ways of computing the field. On the left, each vessel gets its own field direction, which is what happens in a real brain, since vessels point every which way. The patterns point in different directions, and the blood inside each vessel sits at a different field. On the right is 3B0. It's a 2D shortcut that averages the field over three directions to mimic vessels pointing every which way. Every pattern now points the same way, and the blood in every vessel sits at the same offset, close to zero. If all the blood sits at the same offset, there's no spread between vessels to dephase, so the blood signal hardly decays. In my runs it decays at 2 to 3 per second, versus about 42 in the 3D reference. Berman shows the same thing. That sounds small, because blood is only a few percent of the voxel. But blood normally decays so fast that it carries at least a quarter of the gradient-echo decay. Berman found about 25 percent at 2 percent blood volume, and in my runs it's over half for capillary-sized vessels. So the whole signal is off, and so is anything you fit from it.
