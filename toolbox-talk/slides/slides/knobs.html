Then I started playing with it on our own sequence: the 38 GESFIDE echoes, with the 180 pulse at 50 milliseconds and the spin echo at 100. On the left I change oxygenation. Less oxygen means more magnetic blood, so the signal decays faster. That's the BOLD effect. On the right I change vessel size, and the shape changes. Big vessels behave like a static field, so the 180 pulse brings a lot of signal back at the spin echo. Small vessels don't refocus, because water diffuses past them too fast. That difference in shape is what lets fingerprinting tell vessel sizes apart. A nice detail: I only ran one simulation per vessel size and voxel. Oxygenation just scales the field, so one run gives every SO2 for free. Jack's notebook shows that trick, and it makes the dictionary much cheaper. If someone asks why most curves peak a bit before 100 milliseconds: diffusion causes some loss that the 180 can't undo, and that loss keeps growing, so the maximum shifts earlier. The 30 micron curve, where diffusion matters least, peaks closest to 100.
