Now what I actually ran. The toolbox only works with Python 3.10, so I made a separate environment for it. Then I ran Jack's install check, the example script that comes with the toolbox, and Jack's GESFIDE notebook from start to end. The runs are seeded, so the numbers should repeat exactly, and mine matched Jack's. A few examples on the right: the field next to a vessel matches the textbook formula. Water spreads 39.9 square microns in 20 milliseconds, against 40 from theory. The GESFIDE fit on the reference voxel gives R2 of 14.8 and R2 prime of 1.81 per second. And the last row is a preview of the problem. In Jack's head-to-head on our sequence, one voxel each, the blood part of the signal from the MrVox-like 3B0 voxel has an NRMSD of 94 percent against the 3D reference. That's Berman's error measure, the RMS difference relative to the reference's spread. The 2D version with its own field direction per vessel is at about 11 percent.
