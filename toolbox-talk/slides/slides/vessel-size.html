Next I wanted to see whether Berman's result holds on our sequence. These are Boxerman plots, the classic check: how fast the signal decays as a function of vessel size. On the left is the gradient-echo part, at about 30 milliseconds. The 3D reference, in black, and the 2D version with its own direction per vessel, in blue, sit on top of each other. 3B0, in red, sits well below: about three quarters of the reference for large vessels, and much less for small ones. On the right is the spin echo, at 100 milliseconds. It peaks around capillary size, which is the classic result, and here the three are much closer, with 3B0 a bit low for small vessels. So the problem is in the gradient-echo part, which is exactly the part GESFIDE samples in the first 50 milliseconds. Same pattern as Berman's Figure 8C, now with our timing.
