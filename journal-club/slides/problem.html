The paper starts with a practical problem. Each standard MRI method for brain physiology covers only one part of the picture. DSC gives blood volume, blood flow and transit time, but it needs a gadolinium bolus, which limits it in patients with kidney problems. ASL measures blood flow without contrast, but the signal is small, so SNR and resolution are low. DCE measures leakage across the blood-brain barrier, again with gadolinium. So to get a full picture, you stack several scans. That means a longer exam, possible misregistration between scans, and data that are hard to combine. [click] Physiological MRF tries to get several of these parameters from one acquisition, matched against one model.
