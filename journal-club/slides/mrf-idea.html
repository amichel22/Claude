MRF lets the signal vary on purpose

Concept: Ma et al., Nature 2013 (ref. 14 in Lin et al.) · Animation: simulated inversion-recovery FISP train, 3 T tissue values

3

Briefly, since this part is familiar: fingerprinting comes from Ma and colleagues in 2013. Instead of fixed settings, MRF changes the flip angle and the repetition time at every time point. In the animation, the top two strips are the flip angle and the repetition time. Below them are simulated signals for white matter, gray matter and CSF. Each tissue has its own T1 and T2, so each one traces a different curve. That curve is the fingerprint. To read it, you simulate the same train for many T1 and T2 values and look for the closest curve.
