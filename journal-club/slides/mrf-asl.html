Starting with MRF-ASL. This figure is adapted from Su and colleagues. In panel A, label and control blocks come in a pseudo-random order. In panel B, the labeling duration gets shorter across the scan. Together, one scan samples hundreds of effective delays, instead of the handful you get from multi-delay ASL. Panel C shows the models. The one-compartment model fits B1, CBF, tissue T1 and tissue arrival time. The two-compartment model adds arterial arrival time, blood volume and travel time. Wright and colleagues mapped CBF and arrival times in 2.5 minutes, and their CBF agreed with pseudo-continuous ASL, or pCASL. The bar chart is test-retest data from a later Su paper, about 20 days apart on average. B1 and T1 are very repeatable, above 0.96. Arrival time and CBF are lower, at 0.86 and 0.75. As the paper notes, the parameters that rely on the kinetic model and the small ASL signal are the least repeatable.
