The third family, MRF-VFD, looks at exchange across the blood-brain barrier. Panel A, from Venugopal and colleagues, is a hybrid sequence: a gradient-echo readout, a refocusing pulse, then a spin-echo readout. Panel B is a model from Thomson and colleagues: a blood compartment and a tissue compartment that exchange water. The key parameter is tau b, how long water stays in a vessel before it crosses the barrier. On the right are Thomson's maps, without contrast, in three healthy volunteers. Gray matter has higher CBV than white matter, and tau b and blood T1 look similar across regions. With contrast, MacAskill's DCE-MRF cut the variability of T1 over time two- to three-fold compared with standard DCE. That was in mice with breast tumors, so it's a tumor perfusion result rather than a brain one. Acquisition choices also involve trade-offs. Van Dorth's simulations suggested that skipping the preload dose helps permeability estimates but can make blood volume less accurate.
